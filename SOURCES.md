# Catalog sources

Clues are concise paraphrases. Dates refer to the **event written beside them**,
not the earliest use or invention of a unit. The catalog links a relevant source
from each result and reference entry. This is a curated collection, not an
exhaustive inventory of every historical unit or prefixed combination.

## SI and metric

- [BIPM SI Brochure](https://www.bipm.org/en/publications/si-brochure): the seven
  base units, 22 named derived units, definitions, symbols, and accepted non-SI units.
- [NIST SP 330, Appendix 1](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-330/sp-330-appendix-1):
  official CGPM/CIPM decisions and historical dates. Electrical definitions were
  ratified in 1948; SI inclusion in 1960 is not a claim that those units were invented then.
- Pascal and siemens names: 1971. Becquerel and gray: 1975. Sievert: **1979**
  (some accounts cite the proceedings' 1980 publication). Katal: 1999, Resolution 12.
- Metre: 1983 light-speed definition. Kilogram: 1889 prototype adoption. Second:
  1967 atomic definition. Kelvin: 1967 current name. Candela: 1979 radiometric definition.
  These historical milestones do not replace the modern SI definitions.
- Celsius: 1948 name adoption. Radian/steradian: 1995 reclassification as derived
  units; the dates do not claim the concepts began then.
- Litre: 1964 restoration of the cubic-decimetre definition. Hectare and tonne:
  1879 CIPM unit/symbol adoption. Metric carat: 1907 adoption, 200 mg.
- [NIST conversion factors](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-appendix-b-conversion-factors):
  conversions for metric, engineering, and customary units. Thermochemical calorie
  means 4.184 J, not the nutritional kilocalorie or International Table calorie.
- [1954 standard atmosphere resolution](https://www.bipm.org/en/committees/cg/cgpm/10-1954/resolution-4):
  exactly 101,325 Pa.

## Imperial and US customary

- [NBS 1959 yard/pound announcement](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2017/05/09/frn-59-5442-1959.pdf)
  and [USMA transcription](https://usma.org/laws-and-bills/refinement-of-values-for-the-yard-and-the-pound):
  exact international yard (0.9144 m) and pound (0.45359237 kg), and their derived units.
  Dates shown for inches, feet, miles, ounces, stone, tons, psi, and US liquid measures
  identify the modern length/mass basis, not the much older units' origins.
- [NIST Handbook 44, Appendix B](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2024/01/14/NIST%20HB%2044%202024%20Appendix%20B%20Units%20and%20Systems%20of%20Measurement.pdf)
  and [Appendix C](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2024/01/14/NIST%20HB%2044%202024%20Appendix%20C%20General%20Tables%20of%20Units%20of%20Measurement.pdf):
  regional differences, 1824 imperial capacity system, mass/volume conversion tables.
- Length entries use international definitions, not the retired US survey foot.
  Pound/ounce are avoirdupois mass. Short ton = 2,000 lb; long ton = 2,240 lb.
- US gallon/pint/fluid ounce are **liquid** units; dry measures are not aliases.
  Imperial gallon = 4.54609 L, divided into 160 imperial fluid ounces. US gallon =
  231 cubic inches, divided into 128 US fluid ounces. Abbreviations include region
  labels so symbols are unambiguous within this game.
- [Fahrenheit history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit): 1724 scale proposal.
  [Rankine history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rankine_scale): 1859 scale proposal.
- [Mechanical horsepower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsepower): 550 ft·lbf/s,
  approximately 745.7 W. Not metric or electrical horsepower. Origin is given as
  late 18th century rather than an unjustifiably exact year.

## Other fields

- [International nautical mile](https://usma.org/laws-and-bills/adoption-of-international-nautical-mile):
  1,852 m standardized in 1929; knot uses this distance per hour. The US adopted it in 1954.
- [Dyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyne) and [erg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erg):
  BAAS committee naming recommendations in 1873. 10⁻⁵ N and 10⁻⁷ J respectively.
- [IAU: Measuring the Universe](https://iau.org/public/themes/measuring): astronomical
  unit fixed at 149,597,870,700 m in 2012. Light-year uses a Julian year of 365.25 days.
- [IAU 2015 constants discussion](https://doi.org/10.3847/0004-6256/152/2/41):
  parsec = 648,000/π au; this exact relation is stated in the 2015 resolution notes.
- [Nature: A bit on the bit](https://www.nature.com/articles/nphys3878): 1948 use of
  “bit” in Shannon's paper (attributed to Tukey); “byte” coined at IBM in 1956.
  The relation clue uses modern eight-bit bytes, not all historical hardware sizes.
- [Dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_(unit)): unified carbon-12 scale, 1961.
  Electronvolt: exact joule conversion follows the 2019 fixed elementary charge.
- Minute, hour, day, and angular degree have ancient origins. No precise origin
  year is invented. A day here is the 86,400-second duration unit, not every civil day.

## Expanded imperial and historical shelf

- [NIST Handbook 44 (2026), Appendix C](https://www.nist.gov/document/2026-nist-handbook-44-appendix-c):
  troy ounce, grain, pennyweight, apothecaries scruple/dram, avoirdupois dram,
  hundredweights, land measures, and bushels. Troy mass is not avoirdupois mass;
  apothecaries dram = 60 grains, versus 27.34375 grains for avoirdupois dram.
  US bushel is a dry-volume unit. Imperial bushel = eight imperial gallons.
- [NIST SP 811, Appendix B.8](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-appendix-b-conversion-factors/nist-guide-si-appendix-b8):
  pound-force, foot-pound force, International Table Btu, and CGS conversions.
  Btu uses the **IT** variant, not a thermochemical or temperature-specific variant.
- [Slug and slinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slug_(unit)) and
  [poundal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poundal): slug/slinch are masses;
  poundal/pound-force are forces. Slug's name predates 1900; an exact invention
  year is not claimed. Slinch is an informal name for lbf·s²/in, equal to 12 slugs.
- [Gunter's chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunter%27s_chain): 1620 introduction.
  The catalog uses current international-foot conversions for chain, rod,
  furlong, fathom, and acre, not US survey-foot variants. The 2023 clues refer
  explicitly to retirement of the US survey-foot basis, not creation of the units.
- Poise, stokes, gauss, maxwell, oersted, gal, phot, and stilb follow NIST's
  CGS tables. Electromagnetic relations are labeled **correspondences** with SI
  because the systems' dimensional conventions differ. Darcy is permeability;
  debye is electric dipole moment. Both are sometimes written D, so the shelf
  qualifies darcy as `D (permeability)` and reserves bare D for debye.
- [NASA CGS table](https://caps.gsfc.nasa.gov/simpson/ref/gaussian.html): barye.
- [ORAU radiation terminology history](https://orau.org/health-physics-museum/articles/selected-radiological-nuclear-terms.html):
  curie (1910), radiation rad (1953), and barn (1942). Barn is reaction cross
  section, not simply the physical area of a nucleus.
- [ICRU history](https://www.icru.org/current-activities-of-icru/radiation-science-quantities-units-and-radiation-measurement):
  roentgen (1928). [Rem history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roentgen_equivalent_man):
  first definition in 1947; today's conversion is 0.01 Sv. Radiation rad uses
  the NIST-permitted alternative symbol `rd` to avoid confusion with radian `rad`;
  rod's label is qualified as `rd (length)`.
- [Ångström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angstrom): 1868 solar spectrum scale.
  Torr uses 1/760 of the standard atmosphere, not an exact equivalence to every
  millimetre-of-mercury convention.
- [Cubit study](https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/489757): Egyptian royal cubit around
  0.524 m, with surviving rods differing. No universal exact cubit is asserted.
- [English ell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ell), [Paris toise](https://www.sizes.com/units/toise.htm),
  [verst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verst), [arshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arshin),
  and [pood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pood): the named regional conventions,
  not every unit sharing those names. Historic metric equivalents are approximate.
- [Réaumur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9aumur_scale): 1730 proposal.
  [Delisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delisle_scale): 1732 introduction; the
  relation uses the later revised scale (boiling water 0, freezing water 150).
- [Smoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoot): 1958 MIT bridge measurement,
  nominally 5 ft 7 in. [Fortnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnight): 14 days.
  Ancient and undated origins remain explicitly undated instead of assigning
  arbitrary establishment years.
